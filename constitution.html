The Constitution

Nexus Bookclub and Society — Constitution and Bylaws

NEXUS BOOKCLUB AND SOCIETY

CONSTITUTION AND BYLAWS

Adopted by the founding membership

Effective date: ______________________

PREAMBLE

We, the members of Nexus Bookclub and Society, in order to cultivate a durable community of readers; to provide a fair, transparent, and predictable framework for governance; to protect the rights and dignity of every member; and to ensure the Society can adapt to circumstances both favorable and adverse, do hereby establish this Constitution as the founding and governing document of the Society.

Where this Constitution is silent, the Executive Committee shall act in the spirit of fairness, transparency, and the best interests of the membership as a whole.

ARTICLE I — NAME, PURPOSE, AND VALUES

1.1 Name

The organization shall be known as “Nexus Bookclub and Society” (hereafter “the Society”), and may be referred to informally as “Nexus.”

1.2 Purpose

The Society exists to bring readers together for shared discovery, rigorous and generous discussion, and mutual respect across differing interpretations and tastes.

  • Host regular meetings for the discussion of selected books and other literary works.
  • Encourage exposure to diverse genres, authors, cultures, and perspectives.
  • Foster a welcoming environment in which members may express honest views without fear of ridicule or exclusion.
  • Support literacy, reading culture, and, where resources allow, community literary initiatives.
  • Maintain the long-term institutional continuity of the Society independent of any single member.

1.3 Values

The Society is committed to intellectual honesty, pluralism of thought, courtesy in disagreement, and stewardship of the community's shared resources and reputation. No member's continued participation shall depend upon agreement with the majority's literary or personal opinions.

1.4 Non-Affiliation

The Society is an independent, member-run body. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or acting on behalf of any publisher, author, retailer, or media franchise unless a written partnership agreement has been separately ratified under Article XII.

ARTICLE II — MEMBERSHIP

2.1 Eligibility

Membership is open to any individual who supports the Society's purpose, agrees to abide by this Constitution and the Code of Conduct (Article VIII), and completes the application process then in effect.

2.2 Minors

Applicants under the age of 18 may be admitted only with written guardian consent and shall be subject to any additional safeguarding measures adopted by the Executive Committee.

2.3 Categories of Membership

1. Full Member — regular attendee in good standing, with full voting rights.
2. Associate Member — reduced-commitment status (e.g., remote or occasional attendees); may attend and speak but votes only on matters specified in 2.3(a) of the bylaws the Committee adopts.
3. Honorary Member — conferred by two-thirds vote of Full Members for extraordinary contribution; non-voting unless separately holding another category.
4. Founding Member — original signatories to this Constitution; carries no additional voting weight but is recorded for institutional history.

2.4 Rights of Members

  • To attend meetings, nominate and vote on book selections, and participate in discussion.
  • To stand for elected office, subject to Article IV.
  • To inspect the Society's financial summary and meeting minutes upon reasonable request.
  • To raise grievances under Article X without retaliation.

2.5 Obligations of Members

  • To pay dues, if any, as set under Article VII.
  • To comply with the Code of Conduct.
  • To treat fellow members, guests, and any partner institutions with courtesy.

2.6 Resignation

A member may resign at any time by written notice to the Secretary. Resignation does not entitle the member to a refund of dues already paid, except as the Treasurer may authorize on a pro-rated basis for extraordinary circumstances (see 7.6).

2.7 Lapse and Reinstatement

Membership lapses automatically after three consecutive unexcused absences from scheduled meetings, or after non-payment of dues for one full cycle, following written reminder. A lapsed member may seek reinstatement through the standard application process and is not entitled to retroactive rights or standing.

2.8 Suspension and Expulsion

Suspension or expulsion may be imposed only through the disciplinary process in Article IX. No member shall be expelled solely for the content of their literary opinions, criticism of the Society expressed through proper channels, or protected characteristics.

ARTICLE III — GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE

3.1 Executive Committee

The Society shall be governed by an Executive Committee (“the Committee”) consisting of the following officers, each serving one vote on Committee matters:

  • President — presides over meetings, represents the Society externally, and holds the tie-breaking vote (3.4).
  • Vice President — assists the President and assumes presidential duties in the President's absence or incapacity.
  • Secretary — maintains records, minutes, membership rolls, and official correspondence.
  • Treasurer — manages dues, accounts, budgets, and financial reporting.
  • Programming Coordinator — administers the book selection process and meeting schedule.
  • Membership & Conduct Officer — processes applications, monitors compliance with the Code of Conduct, and serves as first point of contact for grievances.

3.2 Committee Meetings

The Committee shall meet no less than quarterly. A majority of Committee seats constitutes quorum. Decisions are made by simple majority of those present unless this Constitution requires a higher threshold.

3.3 Committee Authority

The Committee may act on routine and operational matters between general meetings but may not amend this Constitution, expel a member outright without due process, or commit the Society to expenditure beyond the threshold set in Article VII without membership or budgetary authorization.

3.4 Tie-Breaking and Deadlock

In the event of a tied Committee vote, the President casts the deciding vote. If the President is absent, conflicted, or the tie involves the presidency itself, the Vice President breaks the tie. If both are unavailable or conflicted, the matter is deferred to the next General Meeting for a membership vote.

3.5 Conflicts of Interest

An officer with a direct personal, financial, or familial interest in a matter before the Committee shall disclose the interest and recuse themselves from the vote, though they may participate in discussion at the Committee's discretion.

ARTICLE IV — ELECTIONS AND TERMS

4.1 Term Length

Officers serve one-year terms, renewable by re-election, with a recommended (non-binding) guideline that no officer hold the same seat for more than three consecutive terms, to encourage institutional renewal.

4.2 Nomination

Any Full Member in good standing for at least three months may be nominated, including self-nomination, no fewer than 14 days before the annual election meeting.

4.3 Voting

Officers are elected by a simple majority of Full Members present and voting at the annual meeting, by secret ballot if any member requests it. Associate and Honorary Members do not vote in officer elections unless the bylaws provide otherwise.

4.4 Vacancies

If an officer resigns, is removed, or becomes permanently unable to serve, the Committee shall appoint an interim replacement from among Full Members within 21 days, to serve until the next scheduled or emergency election. If the presidency itself becomes vacant, the Vice President automatically assumes the role on an interim basis.

4.5 Removal from Office

An officer may be removed for cause (material breach of this Constitution, financial misconduct, or serious violation of the Code of Conduct) by a two-thirds vote of Full Members at a meeting for which the proposed removal was on the published agenda, following an opportunity for the officer to respond.

4.6 Contested or Deadlocked Elections

If an election ends in a tie between candidates, a runoff vote shall be held immediately among those tied. If the runoff also ties, the outgoing officer for that seat casts the deciding vote, or, if the seat is newly created or the outgoing officer is one of the tied candidates, the President (or Vice President, per 3.4) decides.

ARTICLE V — MEETINGS

5.1 Types of Meetings

  • Regular Meetings — for book discussion, held on a schedule set by the Programming Coordinator.
  • General Meetings — for governance business, elections, and votes, held at least twice yearly.
  • Emergency Meetings — called by the President, or by petition of at least 20% of Full Members, for urgent matters.

5.2 Notice

Members shall receive at least seven days' notice of Regular Meetings and at least fourteen days' notice of General Meetings, including any agenda items requiring a vote. Emergency Meetings require at least 48 hours' notice except where immediate action is necessary to prevent serious harm to the Society, in which case the Committee shall document the reason for shortened notice.

5.3 Quorum

Quorum for a General Meeting is the greater of ten Full Members or 30% of total Full Membership. If quorum is not met, the meeting may proceed for discussion purposes only; any votes taken must be ratified at a subsequent properly-quorate meeting or by written ballot circulated to the full membership.

5.4 Virtual and Hybrid Meetings

Meetings may be held in person, virtually, or in hybrid format at the Committee's discretion. Members attending virtually count toward quorum and voting where the platform used allows reasonably reliable identification of attendees.

5.5 Voting Procedures

Except where this Constitution specifies a higher threshold, decisions are made by simple majority of votes cast. Abstentions are not counted for or against. Proxy voting is permitted only if the bylaws expressly authorize it and the proxy is submitted in writing before the vote.

5.6 Minutes

The Secretary shall record minutes of every General and Committee meeting, including votes taken and their outcomes, and shall make them available to members within 14 days.

ARTICLE VI — BOOK SELECTION AND DISCUSSION

6.1 Nomination of Titles

Any member may nominate a title for a future meeting. The Programming Coordinator shall maintain a running list and ensure a reasonable diversity of genre, form, author background, and difficulty across a given cycle (recommended: no more than two consecutive selections by authors of the same nationality or in the same genre, absent membership agreement otherwise).

6.2 Selection Method

Titles are selected by member vote (plurality or ranked-choice, as the Committee designates for a given cycle) at least four weeks before the scheduled discussion, to allow reading time.

6.3 Content Advisories

Where a selected work contains graphic violence, sexual content, or other material some members may find distressing, the Programming Coordinator shall issue a brief advisory in the meeting notice. This does not remove the title from selection; it ensures informed attendance.

6.4 Discussion Conduct

Discussion should proceed under a presumption of good faith. Disagreement with a book, an author, or a fellow member's interpretation is expected and welcome; personal attacks, mockery of a member's reading level or background, and deliberate spoilers without warning are not.

6.5 Facilitator Role

A discussion facilitator (the Programming Coordinator or a designated member) may set ground rules for a given session, including time limits per speaker, to ensure balanced participation.

ARTICLE VII — FINANCES

7.1 Dues

The Committee may set annual or periodic membership dues, subject to ratification by simple majority at a General Meeting. Dues levels shall be published at least 30 days before taking effect.

7.2 Financial Hardship

No otherwise-eligible applicant shall be excluded from membership solely for inability to pay dues. The Treasurer may grant fee waivers or reductions at their discretion, subject to Committee review, and the identity of any member receiving a waiver shall remain confidential.

7.3 Budget and Approval Thresholds

The Treasurer shall present an annual budget for Committee approval. Any single expenditure exceeding an amount set in the bylaws (or, absent a bylaw, exceeding 15% of the Society's liquid funds) requires prior approval by General Meeting vote rather than the Committee alone.

7.4 Financial Reporting

The Treasurer shall present a financial summary at every General Meeting and an annual statement of income, expenses, and reserves. Any member may request an independent review of the accounts by petition of 25% of Full Members, at the Society's expense if irregularities are found, and at the petitioners' expense if none are found.

7.5 Custody of Funds

Society funds shall be held in an account requiring at minimum the co-authorization of two officers (typically the Treasurer and the President or Vice President) for any withdrawal or transfer above a nominal petty-cash threshold set in the bylaws.

7.6 Refunds

Dues are generally non-refundable. The Treasurer may authorize pro-rated refunds in cases of serious hardship, relocation, or Society-caused inability to deliver services (e.g., prolonged suspension of meetings), subject to Committee ratification.

7.7 Prohibition on Personal Benefit

No officer or member shall derive personal financial benefit from Society funds beyond reimbursement of properly authorized expenses. Any commercial partnership (Article XII) involving payment to the Society shall be disclosed in the annual financial statement.

ARTICLE VIII — CODE OF CONDUCT

8.1 General Standard

Members shall treat one another with courtesy, respect differing opinions and backgrounds, and refrain from harassment, discrimination, intimidation, or disruptive conduct at any Society function, in person or online.

8.2 Harassment and Discrimination

Harassment, including unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic, sexual harassment, stalking, or threats, is prohibited absolutely and is grounds for immediate suspension pending investigation under Article IX, regardless of the harasser's standing or office.

8.3 Confidentiality of Personal Disclosures

Discussion of books may lead members to share personal experiences. Such disclosures shall not be repeated outside the meeting without the member's consent, absent a safeguarding concern requiring escalation.

8.4 Intellectual Honesty

Members presenting critical or biographical material as fact should make a reasonable effort toward accuracy; honest error is not misconduct, but knowing deception intended to mislead the group is.

8.5 Use of Society Name and Platforms

No member may speak on behalf of the Society to media, partners, or the public without Committee authorization, nor use Society channels (mailing lists, social accounts) for personal, political, or commercial promotion unrelated to Society business.

8.6 Alcohol, Venue, and Guest Conduct

Where meetings are held at venues serving alcohol or in private homes, members are expected to comply with venue rules and host reasonable requests. Guests brought by members are the responsible member's guests for conduct purposes.

ARTICLE IX — DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURES

9.1 Reporting

Any member may report a suspected violation of the Code of Conduct to the Membership & Conduct Officer, or to any other officer if the Conduct Officer is the subject of the complaint.

9.2 Interim Measures

Where a complaint alleges harassment, safety risk, or serious misconduct, the Committee may impose an immediate interim suspension pending investigation, without prejudging the outcome.

9.3 Investigation

The Committee (excluding any officer who is a party to or has a conflict regarding the complaint) shall investigate within a reasonable time, which may include hearing from the complainant, the respondent, and relevant witnesses. Both parties shall be treated with confidentiality to the extent possible.

9.4 Graduated Response

  1. Informal resolution or mediation (Article X), where appropriate and both parties consent.
  2. Verbal or written warning.
  3. Temporary suspension of membership.
  4. Removal from office (per 4.5), independent of continued membership.
  5. Expulsion from the Society.

9.5 Standard of Decision

Disciplinary decisions shall be made on the balance of probabilities (more likely than not), documented in writing, and communicated to the respondent with reasons.

9.6 Appeal

A respondent may appeal a Committee disciplinary decision to the General Membership by written request within 14 days. An appeal is decided by simple majority vote at the next General Meeting, except that expulsion decisions require a two-thirds vote to overturn, reflecting the Committee's closer proximity to the facts.

9.7 False Complaints

A complaint made in good faith but not ultimately upheld is not itself misconduct. A complaint knowingly fabricated to harm another member is a separate violation of this Code.

ARTICLE X — GRIEVANCE AND DISPUTE RESOLUTION

10.1 Scope

This Article governs disputes not involving alleged Code of Conduct violations (Article IX) — for example, disagreements over book selection, event planning, or interpersonal friction that has not risen to misconduct.

10.2 Informal Resolution

Members are encouraged to raise concerns directly and informally in the first instance.

10.3 Mediation

Where informal resolution fails, either party may request mediation by a neutral officer or member agreed upon by both parties. Mediation outcomes are non-binding unless both parties agree in writing to be bound.

10.4 Committee Referral

Unresolved disputes affecting the Society's operation may be referred to the Committee for a binding decision, subject to appeal to the General Membership by simple majority.

10.5 External Disputes

Disputes involving external parties (venues, vendors, partner organizations) shall be handled by the President and Treasurer jointly, with recourse to ordinary legal process only as a last resort and only upon Committee authorization.

ARTICLE XI — RECORDS, COMMUNICATION, AND PRIVACY

11.1 Official Records

The Secretary shall maintain the Constitution, meeting minutes, membership roll, and correspondence archive, retained for no less than seven years and transferred in full to any successor Secretary.

11.2 Data Privacy

Personal information collected for membership purposes (contact details, dues records, disciplinary history) shall be used solely for Society administration, shall not be sold or shared with third parties without consent, and shall be deleted or anonymized within a reasonable period after a member's departure, except where retention is necessary for legal, financial, or safeguarding reasons.

11.3 Official Channels

The Society shall designate official communication channels (e.g., a mailing list or messaging group). Announcements of votes, dues changes, or disciplinary policy shall be made through official channels to ensure all members receive equal notice.

ARTICLE XII — EXTERNAL RELATIONS AND PARTNERSHIPS

12.1 Author and Venue Partnerships

The Committee may pursue relationships with authors, publishers, bookstores, libraries, or venues to support the Society's purpose, provided any agreement involving Society funds or use of the Society's name is disclosed to the membership.

12.2 Sponsorship

Sponsorship or donations from commercial entities require Committee approval and shall not compromise the Society's editorial independence in book selection or discussion.

12.3 Media and Public Statements

Only the President, or a member the President expressly designates, may issue public statements on the Society's behalf.

ARTICLE XIII — CONTINGENCIES AND EXTRAORDINARY CIRCUMSTANCES

13.1 Purpose of This Article

This Article addresses circumstances the Society hopes never to face, so that the Constitution remains workable under both ordinary and adverse conditions.

13.2 Incapacity or Death of an Officer

If an officer becomes incapacitated or dies, the vacancy provisions of 4.4 apply immediately without requiring fault-finding or delay. The Committee shall extend appropriate condolence or support to the officer's family or the officer as circumstances warrant, at the Society's discretion.

13.3 Force Majeure

Where events beyond the Society's control (public health emergencies, natural disasters, venue unavailability, civil disruption) prevent meetings as scheduled, the Committee may suspend, postpone, or move meetings online without triggering the notice periods of Article V, provided reasonable effort is made to inform members promptly.

13.4 Membership Decline

If Full Membership falls below five for two consecutive scheduled General Meetings, the Committee shall place the Society's continued operation on the agenda of the next meeting, with options including recruitment efforts, merger with a like-minded organization (requiring two-thirds member approval), or dissolution under Article XV.

13.5 Complete Loss of Leadership

If the entire Committee resigns, is removed, or is otherwise unable to serve simultaneously, any three Full Members may jointly call an emergency General Meeting, with 72 hours' notice by any available means, solely to elect an interim Committee.

13.6 Financial Insolvency

If Society liabilities exceed its assets, the Committee shall notify the membership within 14 days, cease discretionary spending, and present a recovery or wind-down plan at an emergency meeting. No officer is personally liable for Society debts incurred in good faith within their authority.

13.7 Reputational or Legal Crisis

In the event of a legal claim against the Society or a matter of serious reputational risk, the Committee may take immediate protective action, including venue changes, interim suspensions under 9.2, or engaging outside counsel, and shall inform the membership as promptly as is legally and practically appropriate.

13.8 Best-Case Growth

Should membership grow substantially beyond the Society's original scale, the Committee may propose, for General Meeting approval, additional officer roles, sub-chapters, or a delegate structure, provided any such expansion is codified as a Constitutional amendment under Article XIV rather than informal practice.

13.9 Ambiguity and Silence

Where this Constitution does not address a specific circumstance, the Committee shall act consistently with the Preamble's stated values, document its reasoning in the minutes, and treat the resolution as precedent subject to formalization at the next opportunity to amend.

ARTICLE XIV — AMENDMENTS

14.1 Proposal

Any Full Member may propose an amendment in writing to the Secretary, who shall circulate it to the membership at least 21 days before the vote.

14.2 Adoption

Amendments require a two-thirds majority of Full Members present and voting at a General Meeting at which quorum is met, or by written ballot of the full membership achieving the same threshold.

14.3 Effective Date

Amendments take effect immediately upon adoption unless the amendment specifies a later date, and shall not retroactively invalidate actions properly taken under the prior text.

ARTICLE XV — DISSOLUTION

15.1 Vote to Dissolve

The Society may be dissolved by a two-thirds vote of Full Members at a General Meeting called for that express purpose, with at least 30 days' notice.

15.2 Distribution of Assets

Upon dissolution, after settlement of all debts and liabilities, remaining assets shall be donated to one or more literacy-focused nonprofit organizations or public libraries selected by majority vote of the membership at the dissolution meeting. No asset shall revert to any individual member.

15.3 Records

The Secretary's records shall be archived or transferred to a designated member or institution for historical preservation for no less than seven years following dissolution.

RATIFICATION

This Constitution was adopted by the founding membership of Nexus Bookclub and Society on the date first written above, and remains in force until amended or the Society is dissolved in accordance with its terms.
